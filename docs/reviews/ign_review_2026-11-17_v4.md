# DuffQuest review, take four (Nov 17, 2026)

## It Finally Looks Like One Game. Now It Needs to Feel Alive, and Someone Needs to Find It.

**Score: 8.4 / 10** (v3 was 7.9, v2 7.1, v1 6.4)

Six days ago the street was the weak link: small pixel storefronts between painted rooms. Since then the town went
3D, every storefront and prop was repainted at 4x, all 20+ characters were redrawn at 2x, and today the interface
caught up. The HUD is Duff's phone: battery for energy, signal bars for Madison, a green cash pill, a notification
shade. Every menu, Settings, Gear and the phone itself now speak that one language. For the first time, no screen
looks like it came from a different game.

## Biggest strengths

1. **The writing has a pulse nobody else has.** A line cook in Omaha, rent due Monday, a baby at 3 AM, exes as
   bosses. The jokes are specific ("Left on read. Stings worse than a fryer burn."). Madison's EDS, autism and
   ADHD stories (*The Zebra*, *Too Loud*, *Look At This*) are written from real life with her sign-off, and they
   land as warmth, not a punchline. That is the reason to play.
2. **It's real.** The kitchen games come from a real line: oil held at 350-375°F, allergen cross-contact on the
   lineup quiz, a ticket stolen mid-build, temp-probing the thickest part. Nineteen mini-games now each open with
   a HOW TO PLAY card and an easier first run.
3. **One coherent look.** Painted backdrops, a 2x cast, 3D town with sun shadows, and now a phone-glass UI from
   the HUD to the menus. The new Gear grid (locked items as black silhouettes) and Settings app are better than
   most indie phone ports.
4. **The first five minutes hook.** You open on a porch-pirate fight, then Brittany bets you on Marketplace
   Mike in minute two. Rules come after you've already thrown a punch.
5. **Four side quests** with a journal, markers and choices (cover for a coworker or rat her out), plus real fail
   states that roll you back a day or a week instead of wiping your save.
6. **The pipeline.** Every merge ships to itch and a home-screen web app within minutes, with a 30-day soak test
   and a daily test suite that must pass before anything ships. The title loads in ~9 s on throttled 4G (was 55 s in v1).

## Biggest weaknesses

1. **The town is a beautiful still life.** Nothing moves except Duff and enemies: no cars, no walkers, no birds,
   no weather. Omaha in November with no wind is the first thing that reads as "prototype". (Planned next.)
2. **Nobody can find it.** The itch page copy and art are ready in `docs/itch/PAGE.md` but still need pasting in,
   and play alerts aren't armed. A great game behind a bare page gets a bad game's traffic. This is the cheapest
   big win on the list.
3. **Difficulty is tuned off one player's mood.** Fights went easy -> "too tough" -> halfway back in four days,
   with no data behind any step. It needs two or three outside players before anyone touches a number again.
4. **The second wait.** The title is fast, but Continue and the first arena wait on a ~10 MB pack: about 23 s cold
   on 4G behind a "Loading Omaha..." card. Curious players leave in that window.
5. **Kitchen screens talk too much.** The fryer, sandwich and salad stations carry small text labels ("Sweet spot
   350-375°F", "Too hot = smoke + splatter") that are hard to read on a phone mid-rush. Icons and colour should
   do that work.
6. **Flat cards in a 3D world.** Characters are 2D art standing up in 3D. It works at the default camera, but
   feedback is thin: hits freeze and shake, yet bodies don't squash, swings leave no trail, rolls kick up no dust.
7. **Today's UI hasn't been on a phone yet.** Every screen above was judged from engine renders. Thumb reach on the
   new switches and sheets is unproven.

## Top fixes, ranked by a stranger's first ten minutes

1. **Paste the itch page and arm play alerts.** Costs ten minutes; nothing else matters if no one arrives.
2. **Phone-test today's UI** (Duff's Android + Brittany's iPhone), then two outside players for balance.
3. **Living town + UI motion** (cars, walkers, birds, weather; sliding sheets, squishy buttons, door wipes).
4. **Fight impact** (squash/stretch, swing trails, dust) and the **diorama 3D look** (tilt-shift, lamps that light
   the street).
5. **Cut the second wait:** move the first arena's art into the street pack, or start world.pck during the cold open.

## Closing

v3 said "repaint the walk." That's done, and then some: the walk is 3D and the menus finally match it. What's left
is life (motion in the world, impact in the fists) and eyeballs. Put up the store page this week and this is an
8.5 that people actually play. Add the living town and it's knocking on 9.
